# Master Tech Automotive
## Accident Prevention Program (APP)

**Effective Date:** May 4, 2026
**Revised:** May 4, 2026
**Program Administrator:** [Owner / Shop Manager Name]
**Locations Covered:** Master Tech Automotive — main shop (6 bays)
**Employees Covered:** All employees, including the three technicians, plus any apprentices, helpers, office staff, and temporary workers.

This program is written to comply with **WAC 296-800-140** (Washington State Accident Prevention Program) and to align with **OSHA 29 CFR 1910** general industry standards. Because we have fewer than 11 employees, we use **monthly safety meetings** in place of a formal safety committee, as allowed under **WAC 296-800-13020**.

---

## 1. Safety Policy Statement

Master Tech Automotive is committed to providing a safe workplace. Every employee is expected to follow safe work practices, use required personal protective equipment (PPE), report hazards immediately, and look out for coworkers. No job is so urgent that it must be done unsafely. Management will provide the training, equipment, and time necessary to work safely. Employees who knowingly violate safety rules are subject to discipline, up to and including termination.

## 2. Responsibilities

**Owner / Manager will:**
- Maintain this written program and review it at least annually.
- Provide safety training, PPE, and tools in safe working condition.
- Hold a documented monthly safety meeting.
- Investigate every injury and near-miss.
- Post the **"Job Safety and Health Law"** poster (F416-081-000) where employees can read it.
- Maintain the OSHA 300/300A log if required (auto repair is not exempt — keep injury records).

**Technicians and all employees will:**
- Follow all safety rules and the manufacturer's instructions for tools, lifts, and chemicals.
- Wear required PPE.
- Report injuries, near-misses, and unsafe conditions to the manager immediately.
- Attend safety meetings and training.
- Keep their bay, tools, and walkways clean.

## 3. Safety Orientation for New Employees

Before a new hire performs any work, the manager will walk them through:

1. This Accident Prevention Program and where it is kept (binder by the time clock / digital copy at [location]).
2. How and when to report a work injury (Section 4) and where the first aid kit and eye-wash station are located.
3. How and when to report an unsafe condition (Section 5).
4. Required PPE for their job and how to use, inspect, and store it (Section 6).
5. Emergency action: fire, evacuation, severe injury, chemical spill, earthquake (Section 7).
6. Hazard Communication — location of the SDS binder, how to read a label, and the chemicals used in the shop (Section 8).
7. Specific shop hazards: vehicle lifts, jack stands, compressed air, lithium and 12 V batteries, hot exhaust, refrigerant, used oil, brake dust, solvents, and high-voltage hybrid/EV systems (Section 9).
8. Lockout/Tagout basics for disabled vehicles and shop equipment (Section 10).

The orientation is documented on the **New Employee Safety Orientation Checklist** (Appendix A), signed by the employee and the manager, and kept in the personnel file.

## 4. Reporting Injuries and Getting Medical Care

- **Any injury, no matter how small, must be reported to the manager the same day** it happens.
- For minor injuries (small cut, scrape), use the first aid kit located **[location]**.
- For injuries that need medical care, the designated provider is:
  **[Clinic name, address, phone]** — [hours]
  After-hours / emergencies: **call 911** or go to **[nearest ER, address]**.
- The manager will complete an **Incident Report** (Appendix B) within 24 hours and file the L&I claim through **[www.LNI.wa.gov](https://www.lni.wa.gov)** or the medical provider's Report of Accident form.
- **Fatality, in-patient hospitalization, amputation, or loss of an eye must be reported to L&I within 8 hours** at **1-800-423-7233 (DOSH)**, per WAC 296-800-32005.
- Employees will not be retaliated against for reporting an injury, hazard, or safety concern.

## 5. Reporting Unsafe Conditions

Any employee who sees an unsafe condition, broken tool, damaged lift, leaking hose, missing guard, or unsafe practice must:

1. **Stop using the equipment** and tag it out if it is dangerous.
2. **Tell the manager immediately**, in person, by text, or by writing it on the Hazard Report sheet posted in the break area.
3. The manager will correct the hazard or take it out of service the same day, and document the fix at the next safety meeting.

If the employee believes the hazard is not being corrected, they may contact L&I's Division of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH) at **1-800-423-7233**. This right is protected by law.

## 6. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Master Tech Automotive provides the following PPE at no cost to the employee. The employee is responsible for using, inspecting, and caring for it.

| Task | Required PPE |
|---|---|
| All shop floor work | Safety glasses (ANSI Z87.1), closed-toe leather/safety shoes, no loose jewelry or sleeves |
| Grinding, cutting, drilling, wire-wheeling | Face shield over safety glasses, hearing protection if noisy |
| Welding / torch work | Welding hood (correct shade), welding gloves, leather sleeves/jacket, no synthetic clothing, fire watch for 30 min after |
| Brake / clutch work | N95 (or better) respirator, nitrile gloves, HEPA vacuum or wet method — **no compressed air on brake dust** |
| Battery service (12 V or hybrid/EV HV) | Safety glasses + face shield, nitrile/rubber gloves; for HV pack work: Class 0 (1000 V) lined rubber gloves with leather protectors, insulated tools, HV-rated hot stick, no metal jewelry |
| Solvents, parts cleaning, oils, fuel | Nitrile gloves, safety glasses, apron if splashing likely |
| A/C service | Safety glasses, gloves rated for refrigerant |
| Loud equipment (impact guns, air chisel, lift motor near ear) | Hearing protection (earplugs or muffs) when noise is uncomfortable or > 85 dBA |
| Working overhead | Safety glasses, hard hat when working under a raised vehicle is not possible to avoid head strikes |

PPE is inspected before each use. Damaged PPE is replaced — see the manager.

## 7. Emergency Procedures

**Fire:**
- If small and you are trained: use the nearest ABC fire extinguisher (located **[list locations]**). PASS — Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep.
- If large or spreading: evacuate, pull the alarm if present, **call 911 from outside.**
- Meeting point: **[parking lot location, e.g., far corner of customer lot near the sign].**
- Manager accounts for all employees at the meeting point.

**Severe injury:** Call 911. Do not move the victim unless they are in further danger. Use the first aid kit and bloodborne-pathogen kit. Notify the manager.

**Chemical spill:**
- Small spill (less than 1 gal solvent / oil / coolant): contain with absorbent (located **[location]**), bag in labeled waste, dispose per the SDS and our used-oil/solvent vendor pickup.
- Large spill, fuel, refrigerant release, or unknown chemical: evacuate, ventilate, call 911.

**Earthquake:** Drop, cover, hold under a sturdy bench. Stay away from raised vehicles, lifts, and shelving. Once shaking stops, evacuate to the meeting point and inspect for gas leaks (smell, hissing) before re-entering.

**Power loss with vehicle on lift:** Do not stand under the vehicle. Most two-post and four-post lifts hold position on mechanical locks — verify locks are engaged before any work, every time.

Emergency phone numbers are posted by the shop phone and in the break area.

## 8. Hazard Communication (HazCom — WAC 296-901 / 29 CFR 1910.1200)

- A **Safety Data Sheet (SDS) binder** is kept **[location, e.g., on the shelf above the parts counter]** and a digital copy is at **[link/location]**. SDSs are available to every employee at all times.
- Every chemical container in the shop must be **labeled** with the product name and hazard (the original manufacturer label, or a workplace label with the name and primary hazard for transfer containers like spray bottles).
- A written **chemical inventory** is kept with the SDS binder and updated when new chemicals are brought in.
- New employees are trained on HazCom during orientation; all employees are retrained when a new hazardous chemical is introduced.
- Common chemicals in our shop include: motor oil, gear oil, ATF, brake fluid (DOT 3/4/5.1), antifreeze, gasoline/diesel, parts cleaner / brake cleaner, carb cleaner, penetrating oil, refrigerant (R-134a / R-1234yf), shop solvents, battery acid, RTV/sealants, and aerosol paints.

## 9. Shop-Specific Safe Work Practices

**Vehicle lifts:**
- Only trained employees operate lifts. Inspect daily before use (cables, hoses, locks, arm restraints).
- Set lift pads on the manufacturer's lift points only. Verify the vehicle is stable before going under.
- **Always engage the mechanical safety locks** before working under a raised vehicle.
- ALI-certified annual inspection on every lift, documented and kept on file.
- Never exceed the rated capacity. Watch for load shift on EVs and trucks.

**Jacks and jack stands:** Never work under a vehicle supported only by a hydraulic jack. Always use rated jack stands on a hard, level surface.

**Compressed air:** Shop air must be regulated to **30 psi or less** when used for cleaning, with effective chip guarding and PPE (WAC 296-307-19005 / 29 CFR 1910.242(b)). **Never** use compressed air to blow off skin, clothing, or brake dust.

**Hand and power tools:** Inspect before use. Guards in place. Damaged tools tagged out.

**Hot work (welding, cutting, grinding):** Clear flammables 35 ft. Fire extinguisher within reach. Fire watch 30 minutes after. Never weld or cut on a fuel tank, sealed container, or near a battery.

**Batteries:**
- 12 V: eye protection, no sparks/flames, disconnect negative first, reconnect negative last.
- Hybrid / EV high-voltage (HV): Only trained, qualified technicians work on HV systems. Follow the OEM service-disable procedure, verify zero voltage with a CAT III meter, and use Class 0 insulating gloves with leather protectors. Never work alone on an HV system.

**Used oil, coolant, refrigerant:** Drain into approved containers, store in labeled drums in the bermed area, picked up by **[vendor]**. Refrigerant recovered with a certified machine by an EPA 609-certified tech.

**Brake / clutch dust:** Use a HEPA-filtered vacuum or wet method. **No compressed air, no dry brushing.** (Asbestos exposure rule, WAC 296-62-077.)

**Slip / trip / fall:** Spills cleaned up immediately with absorbent. Hoses and cords routed out of walkways. Bays kept clear at end of shift.

**Lifting and back safety:** Use an engine hoist, transmission jack, or two-person lift for heavy parts. Test the load before lifting. Lift with the legs.

**Working alone:** No HV/EV work, no welding, and no work under a raised vehicle while alone in the shop after hours.

## 10. Lockout / Tagout (LOTO — 29 CFR 1910.147)

Before servicing shop equipment with stored or hazardous energy (air compressor, lift, tire machine, parts washer pump):

1. Notify any affected employees.
2. Shut down the equipment normally.
3. Isolate the energy source (unplug, breaker off, valve closed).
4. Apply a personal lock and tag with the employee's name and date.
5. Verify zero energy by attempting to start the equipment.
6. Service the equipment.
7. Remove tools, replace guards, clear personnel, then remove the lock and restore power.

Each tech is issued a personal padlock and tag. Only the person who applied the lock removes it.

For vehicles being serviced, "lockout" the ignition by keeping the key in the technician's pocket or on a key board controlled by the working tech, and on push-button vehicles, keep the fob outside the vehicle's wake range.

## 11. Monthly Safety Meetings (in lieu of safety committee)

Because we have 10 or fewer employees, we hold a documented **monthly safety meeting** instead of a formal safety committee, per WAC 296-800-13020.

Each meeting will:
- Review any injuries, near-misses, or hazard reports since the last meeting.
- Review one safety topic (rotating: PPE, lifts, fire, HazCom, EV/HV, ergonomics, etc.).
- Take suggestions from employees.
- Be documented on the **Safety Meeting Minutes** form (Appendix C), signed by all attendees, and kept on file for **at least one year**.

---

## 12. Accident / Near-Miss Investigation

Every injury and every near-miss is investigated by the manager, usually within 24 hours, to find the **root cause** — not to assign blame. The investigation is documented (Appendix B) and the corrective action is reviewed at the next safety meeting.

---

## 13. Required Postings

The following are posted in the break area:
- **"Your Rights as a Worker" / Job Safety and Health Law** poster (L&I F416-081-000)
- **Notice to Employees — If a Job Injury Occurs** (L&I F242-191-000)
- Federal and state minimum wage / labor posters
- Emergency phone numbers and the meeting-point map

---

## 14. Program Review

This program is reviewed at least **annually** by the owner, and any time:
- A serious injury or near-miss occurs.
- New equipment, chemicals, or services (e.g., EV/HV repair, ADAS calibration) are added.
- A regulation changes.

Revision history is kept at the back of the binder.
